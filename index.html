Where am I? A robot that finds itself, then drives to the goal.

AbstractThe robot is dropped into a maze with a map but no idea where on it. It explores with a wall follower while a particle filter compares its LiDAR against the map. When the particles collapse into one cluster, a probabilistic roadmap plans a route with A* and pure pursuit drives it. This page runs a JavaScript port of the same four ROS 2 nodes, with the parameters from our launch file. Click either plot to move the goal.

Localization

1000 particles start uniformly over the free space, facing one of four directions. Every 10 steps each one predicts 8 LiDAR ranges and is weighted by a Gaussian with σ = 0.2 m, then stratified resampling keeps the plausible ones. DBSCAN (ε = 0.2) on (x, y, cos θ, sin θ) decides when there is a single answer; after that 25 particles track the pose.

Planning

The roadmap is a 0.2 m grid of nodes that keep 0.15 m from the walls, joined when closer than 0.3 m and the segment stays clear. A* uses straight-line distance as the heuristic. The path is resampled with 5 extra points per edge and smoothed by gradient descent (data weight 0.1, smoothness 0.1).

What can go wrong

The maze is almost symmetric, so once in a while the filter commits to the wrong corridor and “arrives” somewhere else. Figure 1 shows the true error, which the robot never sees. Short lookaheads oscillate and long ones cut corners into walls; try both.
